Every pipefitter needs to do certain calculations repeatedly throughout the day. Here are the 6 most important — with the formulas and a faster way to run them.

## 1. Pipe Offsets

The most common field calculation. For a 45° simple offset:
- **Travel = Spread × 1.414**
- **Set = Spread × 1.000**

For rolling offsets: find the true offset first with `√(Rise² + Roll²)`, then multiply by 1.414.

## 2. Cut Length

Cut length = fitting-to-fitting dimension minus take-outs for each fitting end.

Take-outs vary by fitting type (elbow, tee, flange) and pipe size. Always verify against the manufacturer's dimension tables.

## 3. Pipe Weight

Knowing pipe weight matters for rigging and hanger design.

**Formula:** `Weight (lb/ft) = 10.69 × (OD - Wall) × Wall`

**Example:** 6" Sch 40 (OD=6.625", Wall=0.280")
- Weight = 10.69 × (6.625 - 0.280) × 0.280 = **18.97 lb/ft**

## 4. Thermal Expansion

Pipes grow when heated. Getting this wrong causes stress failures.

**Formula:** `ΔL = α × L × ΔT`

Where:
- α = coefficient of thermal expansion (carbon steel: 6.5 × 10⁻⁶ in/in/°F)
- L = pipe length in inches
- ΔT = temperature change in °F

**Example:** 100-ft carbon steel pipe, ΔT = 200°F
- ΔL = 6.5×10⁻⁶ × 1200 × 200 = **1.56 inches**

## 5. Pipe Support Span

Undersized spans cause sag and overstress. Oversized spans waste materials.

ASME B31.1 and MSS SP-69 give standard spans. For a quick check:
- 4" Sch 40 water-filled: ~13 ft max span
- 6" Sch 40 water-filled: ~17 ft max span
- 8" Sch 40 water-filled: ~19 ft max span

## 6. Barlow's Formula (Min Wall Thickness)

Used to verify a pipe can handle the design pressure.

**Formula:** `t = (P × OD) / (2 × S × E × Y)`

Where:
- P = design pressure (psi)
- S = allowable stress (psi)
- E = longitudinal joint factor
- Y = temperature coefficient
