Getting pipe support spacing right is critical — too far apart and the pipe sags under its own weight, too close and you're wasting materials and installation time.

## The Basic Rule

Maximum span depends on:
- Pipe size and schedule (wall thickness)
- Pipe material (carbon steel, stainless, etc.)
- Contents (gas, water, steam)
- Operating temperature

## MSS SP-69 Standard Spans

These are typical maximum spans for carbon steel pipe with water:

| Pipe Size (NPS) | Sch 40 Max Span |
|---|---|
| 1" | 7 ft |
| 2" | 10 ft |
| 3" | 12 ft |
| 4" | 14 ft |
| 6" | 17 ft |
| 8" | 19 ft |
| 10" | 22 ft |
| 12" | 23 ft |

**For gas service:** increase by ~30% (lighter than water-filled)
**For steam or high-temp:** reduce by 20–30%

## The Formula Behind It

For a simply supported beam with uniform load:

```
L_max = √(10 × S × Z / w)
```

Where:
- S = allowable bending stress (psi)
- Z = section modulus of pipe (in³)
- w = load per unit length (lb/in) — pipe weight + contents

This gets complex fast. That's why calculators exist.

## Hanger Load

Each hanger carries the weight of half the pipe span on each side:

**Hanger Load = w × L**

Where w is lb/ft and L is the tributary length (sum of half spans on each side).
